 Research Profile

I am a Ph.D. candidate in astrophysics at USTC, studying time-domain astrophysics and optical transients, with a particular focus on tidal disruption events (TDEs). My research combines wide-field transient surveys, photometric and spectroscopic follow-up, and multiwavelength analysis to connect transient discovery with physical interpretation.

My future research aims to use TDEs to probe black holes across new mass and redshift regimes, particularly intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs) and high-redshift TDEs. By combining deep surveys, including the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), with rapid follow-up, I aim to uncover faint and poorly sampled transient populations and investigate black-hole demographics and transient accretion physics.

 Research Interests

  • Time-domain astrophysics and optical transients: discovery and physical characterization through wide-field surveys and multiwavelength follow-up.
  • Tidal disruption events: observational diversity, transient accretion, host-galaxy environments, and probes of otherwise quiescent black holes.
  • IMBHs and high-redshift TDEs: extending black-hole searches to new mass, host-galaxy, and redshift regimes.
  • Faint and unexplored transient populations: faint nuclear transients and unusual accretion events accessible to increasingly deep surveys.

 Research & Technical Skills

  • Surveys and transient discovery: transient detection and classification, survey data mining, candidate selection, light-curve analysis, variability characterization, and multiband photometry.
  • Imaging and photometry: CCD calibration, image stacking, astrometry, image subtraction, difference imaging, and photometric measurements.
  • Spectroscopy: optical/UV spectral analysis, continuum and emission-line characterization, and transient classification.
  • SED and physical modeling: multiwavelength spectral energy distribution fitting, blackbody and continuum modeling, Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), and Bayesian inference.
  • Host galaxies: galaxy photometry and morphology, stellar-population analysis, host-galaxy SED fitting, and spectral fitting.
  • Scientific computing: astronomical data analysis, scientific computing, and visualization, primarily in Python.
