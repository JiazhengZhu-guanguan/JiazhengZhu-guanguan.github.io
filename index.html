About Me

Ph.D. Candidate in Astrophysics · USTC

I am a Ph.D. candidate in astrophysics at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), working under the supervision of Prof. Tinggui Wang and Prof. Ning Jiang. My research centers on time-domain astrophysics and optical transients, combining wide-field transient surveys with follow-up observations and multi-wavelength data analysis, with particular expertise in tidal disruption events (TDEs).

I am a member of the WFST TDE working group, and I have hands-on observational experience spanning on-site telescope operations and multi-wavelength follow-up. I have participated in on-site observing runs and survey operations for the Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST), and have been awarded observing time as Principal Investigator for transient follow-up programs on Swift UVOT/XRT and the Gemini Observatory, with additional cooperation on HST, VLA, MMT, P200, XMM-Newton and LCOGT. My work has contributed to the discovery of a featureless TDE at z ~ 1 with WFST, the ultraviolet spectral characterization of the slowly evolving TDE Ansky with quasiperiodic eruptions, and the identification of the faintest and closest optical TDE AT 2023clx.

Looking toward the era of increasingly deep time-domain surveys, particularly the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), I am especially interested in exploring the faint and still poorly sampled regimes of the transient sky. My future research focuses on using TDEs to probe black holes across new mass and redshift regimes — in particular TDEs from intermediate-mass black holes (IMBHs) and high-redshift TDEs.

Featured Work

  AT2025wet: A Tidal Disruption Event with Quasiperiodic Eruptions

Multi-wavelength observations and physical interpretation of the TDE AT2025wet — featured work

Tidal disruption events (TDEs) occur when a star is torn apart by a supermassive black hole, offering a unique window into otherwise quiescent black holes. AT2025wet is a tidal disruption event we have followed up across the optical, ultraviolet, and X-ray bands.

Combining early survey discovery with rapid multi-wavelength follow-up and spectroscopy, we characterize the temporal evolution of the accretion flow and probe the geometry and physics of the disk, contributing to our understanding of the diversity of TDEs and their black-hole demographics.

  • Secured early multi-wavelength follow-up of the TDE AT2025wet.
  • Characterized the light-curve evolution and physical conditions of the accretion flow.
  • Highlights the value of wide-field surveys plus rapid follow-up for TDE science.
