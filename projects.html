Selected first-author work on tidal disruption events, transient accretion, and explosive transients

  AT2025wet: Discovery of a Featureless TDE at z ~ 1 with WFST

Extending optical TDE discovery to z ~ 1 with WFST — ApJL, 1004, L10, 2026   DOI

We discovered WFST250820mmsw/AT2025wet with the 2.5-m Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST), demonstrating that wide-field optical surveys can identify tidal disruption events well beyond the redshift range of most previously known optical samples. The transient is a blue nuclear flare reaching g ~ 22 mag, while absorption features from the host galaxy securely establish a redshift of z = 1.037.

Near maximum light, Keck/LRIS spectroscopy shows a remarkably featureless blue continuum with no obvious broad emission lines. Its luminous and slowly evolving spectral energy distribution is consistent with a featureless TDE, and the paper reported AT2025wet as the highest-redshift non-jetted TDE known at the time. At z ~ 1, optical observations directly probe the rest-frame ultraviolet, opening a new route to constrain the intrinsic TDE SED, the poorly observed extreme-UV peak, and the origin of the optical emission.

  • Extended spectroscopically confirmed optical TDEs to z = 1.037.
  • Identified a luminous featureless TDE from WFST photometry and Keck spectroscopy.
  • Demonstrated the potential of WFST and future deep surveys to build high-redshift TDE samples and probe SMBHs across cosmic time.

  Ansky: UV Spectral Evidence for a Slowly Evolving Featureless TDE with QPEs

Using HST ultraviolet spectroscopy to investigate the nature of the QPE source Ansky — ApJL, 994, L16, 2025   DOI

ZTF19acnskyy (Ansky) is an unusual nuclear transient associated with X-ray quasi-periodic eruptions (QPEs). Its exceptionally slow optical evolution and AGN-like variability had complicated its interpretation. To distinguish between an AGN-related origin and a tidal disruption event, we obtained ultraviolet spectroscopy with the Hubble Space Telescope.

The HST spectrum reveals a featureless ultraviolet continuum without the broad UV or optical emission lines expected from many AGNs. Its steep UV slope is more consistent with TDEs, while its low luminosity and unusually long rise and decay timescales distinguish it from previously known featureless events. Precise astrometry places the transient at the galactic nucleus, with a 3σ offset limit of 54 pc, disfavoring an off-nuclear IMBH interpretation. We therefore proposed a slowly evolving featureless TDE, potentially involving disruption of a post-main-sequence star.

  • Used HST UV spectroscopy to discriminate between TDE- and AGN-like interpretations of Ansky.
  • Established Ansky as an unusually low-luminosity, slowly evolving member of the featureless TDE population.
  • Provided independent evidence strengthening the observational connection between TDEs and QPEs.

  AT 2023clx: The Faintest and Closest Optical TDE

Revealing the faint end of the optical TDE population in nearby galaxy NGC 3799 — ApJL, 952, L35, 2023   DOI

AT 2023clx occurred in the nearby star-forming galaxy NGC 3799 at z = 0.01107 (47.8 Mpc). Its nuclear location, blue nearly constant-temperature continuum, broad Balmer emission, He II λ4686, and approximately t−5/3 decline identify it as a tidal disruption event. With a peak absolute magnitude of only Mg = −17.16 and a peak bolometric luminosity of 4.56 × 1042 erg s−1, it was the faintest and closest optical TDE reported at the time.

Its proximity enabled detailed multiwavelength follow-up despite the intrinsically low luminosity, including a very stringent Swift/XRT nondetection. More importantly, AT 2023clx demonstrates that optically faint TDEs can escape current flux-limited samples. Such events are therefore essential for constraining the faint end of the TDE luminosity function and motivate deeper searches with facilities such as WFST and LSST.

  • Reported an exceptionally nearby and intrinsically faint optical TDE in a star-forming host galaxy.
  • Confirmed the TDE nature through its nuclear position, blue continuum, broad Balmer and He II emission, and light-curve evolution.
  • Highlighted a potentially underexplored population of faint TDEs that deeper time-domain surveys should uncover.

  SN 2017egm: A Helium-rich Superluminous Supernova with Multiple Bumps

Tracing circumstellar interaction in one of the best-observed nearby superluminous supernovae — ApJ, 949, 23, 2023   DOI

SN 2017egm was one of the nearest known hydrogen-poor superluminous supernovae and an unusual event in a massive, metal-rich host. Our spectroscopy revealed strong He I λ10830 emission together with multiple optical He I absorption features, placing SN 2017egm in the emerging class of helium-rich SLSNe-I (SLSNe-Ib). By combining our observations with archival data, we constructed an exceptionally complete UV–optical–NIR light curve extending from before maximum to roughly 300 days after peak.

The bolometric light curve contains several pronounced bumps that are difficult to reproduce with a single smooth power source. Repeated interaction between the ejecta and structured circumstellar material provides a natural explanation for the undulating evolution. A strong infrared excess, plausibly produced by pre-existing or newly formed dust, gives additional support to the interaction picture. Deep Chandra observations further place an unusually stringent limit on late-time X-ray emission, constraining the environment and possible central-engine contribution.

  • Identified SN 2017egm as a helium-rich SLSN-Ib from optical and near-infrared spectroscopy.
  • Built its most complete bolometric light curve and showed that multiple bumps favor repeated ejecta–CSM interaction.
  • Combined the infrared excess and deep X-ray limits to place additional constraints on the circumstellar environment and power source.
