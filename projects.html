First-author and featured works on optical transients and tidal disruption events

  AT2025wet: A Featureless TDE at z ~ 1 discovered with WFST

A tidal disruption event discovered by the Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST) — first-author work

Wide-field time-domain surveys are opening access to tidal disruption events (TDEs) at intermediate and high redshift, probing the demographics of black-hole accretion across cosmic time. AT2025wet was discovered by the Wide Field Survey Telescope (WFST) as a featureless (line-stripped) TDE at z ~ 1.

Featureless TDEs are an emerging class whose ultraviolet spectra show broad, absorption-dominated continua without strong emission lines. Combining survey discovery with rapid follow-up and spectroscopy, we characterize the event's light curve and spectral energy distribution, extending this population to higher redshift and demonstrating the power of wide-field surveys for TDE science.

  • Discovered a featureless TDE at z ~ 1 with WFST.
  • Characterized its multi-band light curve and spectral energy distribution.
  • Extends the featureless TDE population to higher redshift with a wide-field survey.

  Ansky: A Quasiperiodic Eruption source from HST UV observations

HST ultraviolet observations of the QPE source Ansky — first-author work

Quasiperiodic eruptions (QPEs) are periodic X-ray/UV brightening episodes seen in some nuclear transients, possibly produced by an orbiting stellar remnant or a repeating partial disruption. Ansky is a QPE source for which we obtained a rich ultraviolet dataset with the Hubble Space Telescope (HST).

Our HST ultraviolet observations reveal pronounced variability and spectral signatures of the eruption cycles, enabling us to probe the geometry and physics of the accretion flow driving these quasiperiodic eruptions.

  • Studied the QPE source Ansky with HST ultraviolet observations.
  • Characterized the UV variability and spectral evolution of the eruptions.
  • Places constraints on the physics underlying quasiperiodic eruptions.

  AT 2023clx: The Faintest & Closest Optical TDE

A nearby TDE in star-forming galaxy NGC 3799 — ApJL, 952, L35, 2023   DOI

Most optical TDEs are discovered in quiescent, elliptical hosts. AT 2023clx stands out as the faintest and closest optical TDE found to date in a nearby star-forming galaxy (NGC 3799), making it an excellent target for detailed follow-up.

We present a comprehensive multi-wavelength characterization of AT 2023clx, including early rise and decay behavior, and discuss what it reveals about the diversity of TDEs and their host environments, as well as the role of star-forming host galaxies in producing detectable transients.

  • Reported the faintest and closest optical TDE discovered in a star-forming galaxy.
  • Provided well-sampled light curves and multi-band photometry across the outburst.
  • Highlights the diversity of TDE hosts and transient discovery near the survey limit.

  SN 2017egm: A Helium-rich Superluminous Supernova with Multiple Bumps

Precursor activity and light-curve features of a superluminous supernova — ApJ, 949, 23, 2023   DOI

Superluminous supernovae (SLSNe) are among the most energetic explosions in the universe, and the origin of their light-curve bumps remains debated. SN 2017egm is a helium-rich SLSN showing multiple bumps in its light curve and clear precursor activity.

We model the multi-band light curves and spectra, interpreting the bumps as episodes of continued energy injection — possibly from a magnetar or from interaction with circumstellar material. This provides insight into the diversity of engines powering superluminous explosions.

  • Characterized multiple bumps and precursor activity in a helium-rich SLSN.
  • Modeled the light-curve bumps as extra energy-injection episodes.
  • Contributes to our understanding of SLSN powering mechanisms (magnetar vs. interaction).
